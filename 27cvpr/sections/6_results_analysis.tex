\subsection{Future prediction}

\Cref{tab:future_prediction} evaluates four dimensions of the predicted future
state: clinical status, disease progression, report fidelity, and probabilistic
reliability, with two metrics per dimension. Inputs comprise the current radiograph and
report, clinical history available by the source cutoff, and a horizon bin.
The current report is assumed available retrospectively; follow-up images and
reports supply targets only. Four public VLM checkpoints use zero-shot forecast
prompts, with Qwen3.5-9B used for the Qwen reference. BioViL-T and CheXWorld
receive trained forecast modules; Copy Current and a finding-transition prior
provide simple references. The bottom three rows report legacy Qwen3.5-9B full-token,
eight-slot, and cross-patient shuffled-state forecasters. These conditions
complete matched forecast training from a shared 9B Stage~1 initialization
and evaluation on all 297 test pairs, including GREEN. The slot adaptation uses final-language-layer
queries, separate from the proposed multi-depth $4+4$-slot model. Their decoder
reads the predicted representation without the native current-image context.
New native supervised and native decoder plus predicted-state conditions remain
pending; the legacy full-token row is not native Qwen image--text fine-tuning.

Clinical status uses finding macro AP (non-interpolated average precision) and
AUROC. Disease progression uses Transition F1, averaged over supported
finding-specific onset and resolution events, and Direction F1, which measures
improvement, stability, and worsening of persistent findings. Stable references
remain eligible for false-change penalties. RadGraph partial F1 and GREEN assess
the clinical content of generated future reports. Probabilistic reliability uses
macro Brier score and classwise ECE on the same future-finding probabilities.
Zero-shot VLM scores are derived from fixed Yes/No answer likelihoods; trained
forecasters use supervised finding heads. The planned native supervised and
state-augmented comparison will use matched finding heads. All methods share
the reference-defined eligibility masks for each metric; training and readout
differences and calibration rules are specified in the supplementary material.
Direction annotations remain unadjudicated for this cohort, so that column is
pending; the separate 82-pair direction study is not included.

All scores below and in the two tables are multiplied by 100, except PSNR (dB).
Qwen3.5-9B reaches AP 85.55 and AUROC 81.77 without task adaptation; CheXagent
reaches 86.89/81.48. BioViL-T reaches 84.30/81.24 with the shared trained
predictor. Relative to the full-token forecaster, the 9B slots improve
Transition F1 (28.51 versus 16.03) and AUROC (77.94 versus 76.42), with lower
Brier/ECE, but slightly lower AP, RadGraph, and GREEN. Shuffling reduces AUROC
to 52.85 and GREEN to 8.87; all 297 generated reports collapse to one output,
with zero CheXbert F1. Its lower ECE (4.97) therefore accompanies poor
discrimination and report content. These point estimates support a limited
state-condition comparison; they do not validate the proposed multi-depth
model or establish the effect of native Qwen fine-tuning. A future comparison
against native supervised Qwen will test the added state branch as a whole;
current-slot and full-token controls with the same encoders are needed to
separate prediction and compression effects. Historical 0.8B ablation scores
remain archived separately.

\subsection{Downstream tasks}

\Cref{tab:downstream_tasks} covers six tasks without the future predictor:
classification, standard VQA, current report generation, phrase grounding,
organ segmentation, and super-resolution. This expanded image-based evaluation
withholds the same-exam report from state construction. Questions and phrases
condition task readout after the state is encoded. The no-slots baseline and
\method{} remain pending under matched six-task supervision and decoders.
Their earlier report-assisted results and separate two-epoch joint dense-task
pilots do not populate those rows. Frozen Qwen3.5-9B visual-slot task heads,
their shuffled control, and an image-only decoder are reported separately.

Classification uses 13 MIMIC finding/device labels and reports macro AUROC/AP
on 353 test images. DINOv2/CheXWorld classification heads use 13,681 training
and 160 validation images for 20 epochs. Current-report scoring uses 507 images.
MIMIC-CXR-VQA provides standard questions and official Accuracy/micro F1;
Verify/Choose/Query and whole-image/region breakdowns will be reported separately.
Current report generation uses MIMIC-CXR with RadGraph partial F1 and CheXbert
macro-positive F1. MS-CXR phrase grounding uses mIoU and box accuracy at
IoU $\geq0.5$ under one fixed conversion and matching protocol.

Segmentation measures Dice for the lungs and heart against CXAS pseudo-labels
and, separately, human lung masks from Montgomery. Super-resolution uses
synthetic $4\times$ downsampling per spatial dimension, with LR imagery in both
input branches and HR used only as the target. PSNR and SSIM exclude padding.
DINOv2/CheXWorld use frozen visual encoders with supervised heads; MAIRA-2 and
SwinIR provide reporting/grounding and reconstruction references, respectively.
The completed SwinIR adaptation reaches 37.27 dB and SSIM 94.51, but consumes
8.313 GPU hours including evaluation, versus 0.161 for the image-only SR run.
This is not an equal-compute comparison. Formal VQA, MS-CXR grounding, and the
full six-task model rows remain pending; derived QA and anatomical-region
localization scores are excluded.
