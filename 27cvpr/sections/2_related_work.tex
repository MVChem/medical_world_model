\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Clinical Multimodal Representation}
Medical image--text pretraining uses paired images and reports to learn clinical semantics. BioViL demonstrates the value of radiology-specific language modeling for cross-modal alignment and transfer~\citep{boecking2022biovil}. Medical VLMs extend this foundation to instruction following and generation: LLaVA-Med learns biomedical conversations from figure--caption pairs and synthetic instructions, while MAIRA-2 connects report statements to image regions~\citep{li2023llavamed,bannur2024maira2}. M3D and Med3DVLM extend multimodal analysis to volumetric imaging~\citep{bai2024m3d,xin2026med3dvlm}.

These capabilities motivate using a VLM's internal representations as an interface to other predictors. LISA provides a concrete example: the hidden embedding of a segmentation token conditions a mask decoder alongside visual features~\citep{lai2024lisa}. Its token represents a requested segmentation output. Our state is constructed before the task request, with learned readouts of both fused vision--language features and native visual features. Clinical task supervision shapes this shared interface, and spatial decoders combine its visual slots with image evidence. The same state space then defines the targets for longitudinal prediction, connecting multimodal understanding to a reusable predictive representation.

\subsection{Predictive Representation Learning}
Joint-embedding predictive architectures learn representations by predicting latent targets. I-JEPA predicts embeddings of masked image regions, and V-JEPA extends feature prediction to video~\citep{assran2023ijepa,bardes2024vjepa}. VL-JEPA applies the principle across modalities by predicting continuous text embeddings for vision--language tasks~\citep{chen2026vljepa}. VLA-JEPA is a direct architectural precedent: VLM-derived latent action tokens condition a world model that predicts future visual states, with future frames confined to the target pathway~\citep{sun2026vlajepa}. Its tokens encode transition information for robotic control, while the predicted states come from a visual encoder. In \method{}, the predicted object is the multimodal observation state itself, combining fusion and visual slots. We first establish this state through clinical task supervision, then learn transitions from actual follow-ups; the conditioning variable specifies an observation horizon.

Within medical imaging, CheXWorld learns radiographic representations through prediction of local anatomy, global layout, and appearance-domain variations~\citep{yue2025chexworld}. X-WIN learns latent prediction across CT-derived X-ray projections to transfer volumetric anatomical knowledge to radiographs~\citep{yang2026xwin}. Their predictive targets capture spatial structure and acquisition transformations. Our temporal targets instead come from repeated observations of the same patient, requiring a representation of clinical change across examinations.

\subsection{Medical World Models}
Longitudinal medical learning spans interpretation of observed change and prediction of future disease. BioViL-T and TempA-VLP use repeated examinations to learn temporal image--text representations~\citep{bannur2023biovilt,yang2025tempavlp}, while HERGen incorporates imaging history into report generation~\citep{wang2024hergen}. Generative approaches extend this direction to future images: EHRXDiff conditions chest X-ray synthesis on earlier imaging and subsequent medical events, and MedDream simulates disease trajectories through image and video generation~\citep{kyung2025ehrxdiff,cao2026meddream}.

Medical world models connect disease dynamics to clinical decisions. MeWM combines vision--language treatment planning with generative tumor simulation~\citep{yang2025medicalworldmodel}; CLARITY models treatment-conditioned trajectories in latent space with temporal and clinical context~\citep{ding2025clarity}. Clin-JEPA also couples latent EHR trajectory prediction with a reusable backbone for downstream risk tasks~\citep{yang2026clinjepa}. These works establish precedents for both latent patient dynamics and predictive representation learning. Our focus is a shared state of imaging observations and available reports that supports horizon-conditioned follow-up prediction and current clinical and spatial tasks. We therefore assess future prediction and current-state reuse separately, with image evidence retained for dense outputs. This formulation targets observational follow-ups rather than treatment-policy optimization.
