\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Medical world models aim to represent patient states and predict their evolution from clinical observations. Longitudinal medical imaging provides a natural setting for learning such models: successive examinations record findings that may persist, resolve, or progress, while accompanying reports describe their clinical significance. These observations provide complementary evidence about an evolving patient state. Learning to connect them creates an opportunity to bring medical image understanding and future-state prediction into a common framework, in which the interpretation of an examination is informed by the temporal relationships observed across patient histories.

Recent work has begun to explore this direction. MeWM combines treatment planning with generative simulation of tumor evolution, linking anticipated disease changes to the evaluation of clinical actions~\citep{yang2025medicalworldmodel}. CLARITY models treatment-conditioned disease trajectories in latent space, incorporating temporal and clinical context to support treatment decisions~\citep{ding2025clarity}. These approaches demonstrate the relevance of modeling patient evolution to medical decision support. Building on this direction, we focus on learning a shared representation of medical observations whose content supports clinical and spatial understanding and whose dynamics can be learned from actual follow-up examinations. This perspective places the definition of the medical state at the center of world modeling.

Constructing such a state presents a fundamental challenge. Images capture anatomical structures and spatial manifestations of disease, while reports express findings and their clinical interpretation. Across examinations, changes in this evidence reflect both patient evolution and variation in how observations are acquired and described. A useful predictive representation must therefore retain medically relevant information while supporting comparisons across time. We view state construction and temporal modeling as coupled learning problems: the content of a state determines what its dynamics can express, and the demands of temporal prediction help determine which information the state should preserve.

Joint-embedding predictive architectures provide a learning principle for this formulation. I-JEPA learns image representations by predicting target-region embeddings, while V-JEPA learns visual representations through feature prediction in video~\citep{assran2023ijepa,bardes2024vjepa}. These approaches establish prediction in representation space as a means of learning useful visual structure. For longitudinal medicine, this principle motivates predicting the representation of a follow-up examination directly in a shared latent space. The central opportunity is to ground that space in both visual evidence and clinical meaning, so that learning temporal relationships also contributes to a useful representation of medical observations.

We introduce MedWorld-JEPA, a JEPA-style medical world model that jointly learns multimodal patient states and their temporal dynamics. The model constructs a compact state from complementary visual and vision--language representations of an examination. Clinical and spatial tasks shape the information represented in this state, while a horizon-conditioned predictor learns to anticipate the state encoded from a real follow-up image--report pair. Both objectives are optimized jointly, allowing medical understanding and temporal prediction to influence the same representation. At inference, the predictor operates directly in latent space to forecast follow-up states. The observation states also condition image-based decoders for classification, visual question answering, and segmentation.

A shared predictive space also offers a basis for connecting observations across patients. Clinically meaningful relationships in this space could support retrieval of cases with comparable findings, allowing an individual examination to be considered alongside relevant patient histories and their recorded follow-ups. This gives the learned representation a potential role beyond producing an isolated forecast: it could help organize longitudinal observations into an accessible collection of related clinical experiences. We view this connection between patient-state prediction and case retrieval as a practical motivation for learning medical world models whose representations remain useful across tasks.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We introduce a JEPA-style medical world model that learns horizon-conditioned patient-state dynamics directly in a multimodal latent space, using real longitudinal image--report observations.
    \item We develop a shared medical state representation that combines visual and vision--language information, jointly shaped by clinical, spatial, and temporal learning to support both current image understanding and future-state prediction.
\end{itemize}
