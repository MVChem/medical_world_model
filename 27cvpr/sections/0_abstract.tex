Medical world models aim to represent patient states and predict their evolution from clinical observations. A central challenge is to learn a predictive state space that connects clinical semantics with visual evidence. We introduce MedWorld-JEPA, a medical world model that learns multimodal patient states and their temporal dynamics from longitudinal images and reports. Following a JEPA-style architecture, it learns to predict future patient representations directly in latent space, with actual follow-up examinations providing supervision. Our central idea is to jointly learn what constitutes a medically informative state and how that state evolves across examinations, connecting medical image understanding with longitudinal forecasting. The resulting space is designed to capture both temporal relationships within patients and clinically meaningful similarities across patients. Beyond forecasting, such representations provide a foundation for retrieving similar cases and their longitudinal histories, connecting individual observations to relevant clinical experience. MedWorld-JEPA thus offers a framework for integrating the interpretation of current observations with the anticipation of future states, opening a path toward case-based support for longitudinal clinical assessment.
